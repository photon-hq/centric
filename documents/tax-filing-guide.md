# TaxPro Services - Tax Filing Guidelines

## Individual Tax Returns

### What You Need to File

To complete your individual tax return, please gather the following documents:

**Income Documents:**
- W-2 forms from all employers
- 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC for freelance/contract work
- 1099-INT for interest income
- 1099-DIV for dividend income
- 1099-B for investment sales
- K-1 forms for partnership or S-corporation income
- Social Security benefits statements (SSA-1099)
- Unemployment compensation (1099-G)

**Deduction Documents:**
- Mortgage interest statements (1098)
- Property tax records
- Charitable donation receipts
- Medical expense receipts (if itemizing)
- State and local tax payment records
- Student loan interest statements (1098-E)
- Educational expenses (1098-T)

**Personal Information:**
- Social Security numbers for you, spouse, and dependents
- Bank account and routing numbers for direct deposit
- Prior year tax return
- Health insurance coverage information (Form 1095)

### Standard Deduction vs. Itemized Deductions

For tax year 2025:
- Single filers: $15,000 standard deduction
- Married filing jointly: $30,000 standard deduction
- Head of household: $22,500 standard deduction

You should itemize if your total deductions exceed these amounts. Common itemized deductions include:
- State and local taxes (up to $10,000)
- Mortgage interest
- Charitable contributions
- Medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of AGI

### Filing Deadlines

- Regular deadline: April 15
- Extension deadline: October 15 (requires filing Form 4868 by April 15)
- Estimated tax payments: Due quarterly (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15)

**Important:** Filing an extension gives you more time to file, but NOT more time to pay. You must still pay estimated taxes by April 15 to avoid penalties.

## Common Tax Credits

### Child Tax Credit
- Up to $2,000 per qualifying child under 17
- Up to $1,600 is refundable
- Income phase-out begins at $400,000 (married) or $200,000 (single)

### Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)
- For low to moderate income workers
- Amount varies based on income and number of children
- Fully refundable

### Education Credits
- American Opportunity Credit: Up to $2,500 per student for first 4 years
- Lifetime Learning Credit: Up to $2,000 per return for any college or career courses

### Energy Credits
- Residential Clean Energy Credit: 30% of solar panel costs
- Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit: Up to $3,200 for qualifying improvements

## Audit Risk Factors

While audit rates are low (less than 1% for most taxpayers), certain things can increase your risk:

**Higher Risk Items:**
- Very high income (over $500,000)
- Large charitable deductions relative to income
- Home office deduction for employees
- Significant business losses year after year
- Cash-intensive businesses
- Foreign bank accounts not properly disclosed
- Math errors or missing information

**How to Reduce Risk:**
- Keep excellent records
- Report all income (IRS already knows about most of it)
- Be reasonable with deductions
- Use a qualified tax professional
- File electronically to reduce errors

## If You're Self-Employed

### Quarterly Estimated Taxes
If you expect to owe $1,000 or more in taxes, you must make quarterly estimated payments:
- Q1: April 15
- Q2: June 15  
- Q3: September 15
- Q4: January 15 (of following year)

### Self-Employment Tax
You must pay both the employer and employee portions of Social Security and Medicare:
- 12.4% for Social Security (on first $168,600 of net earnings)
- 2.9% for Medicare (on all net earnings)
- Additional 0.9% Medicare tax on earnings over $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married)

### Business Deductions
Common deductions for self-employed individuals:
- Home office (if space is used exclusively for business)
- Business vehicle expenses (actual or standard mileage)
- Equipment and supplies
- Professional services (legal, accounting)
- Business insurance
- Advertising and marketing
- Professional development and education
- Business-related travel and meals (50% for meals)

### Retirement Contributions
Self-employed individuals can deduct contributions to:
- SEP IRA: Up to 25% of net self-employment income (max $69,000)
- Solo 401(k): Up to $23,000 employee deferral + 25% employer contribution
- SIMPLE IRA: Up to $16,000 employee deferral + employer match
